Youth clay target team · Aitkin County, Minnesota

Every shooter counts

The Aitkin County Elite Sharpshooters (ACES) are a youth trap, skeet and sporting clays team for students in grades 5–12, based at the Wealthwood Rod & Gun Club in central Minnesota. We compete in the Scholastic Clay Target Program (SCTP), and we're always looking for new shooters, whether they've never held a shotgun or have shot for years.

Who we are

A team, not just a few top shooters

ACES is a youth shotgun sports team registered with the Scholastic Clay Target Program, made up of students and families from around Aitkin County. Our shooters range from first-year beginners to experienced varsity athletes, and they practice, travel and compete together. Many also shoot for their school's High School Clay Target League team or with 4-H.

Safety first, always

Safe gun handling and range rules come before anything else, at every practice and every competition.

Every shooter counts

We measure ourselves by how the whole team does, so the newest shooter's improvement matters as much as the best shooter's score.

Grow at your own pace

Beginners learn the basics with coaching and patience. Experienced shooters get the competition and training to keep improving.

The program

What is SCTP?

The Scholastic Clay Target Program is a national youth shooting program run by the Scholastic Shooting Sports Foundation (SSSF). Teams across the country practice and compete in clay target shooting, and athletes are grouped by grade so they compete against shooters their own age.

DivisionWho shoots in it
Rookie5th grade and under
IntermediateMiddle school. Entry Level is for newer shooters; Advanced is for those with experience.
SeniorHigh school, grades 9–12, in Junior Varsity or Varsity.

ACES takes shooters in grades 5–12. SCTP sets the exact division rules each year, and our coaches help each athlete get placed correctly.

What we shoot

Trap, skeet and sporting clays are the main events. Experienced shooters can also try handicap trap and doubles.

Where it leads

The season builds toward the Minnesota SCTP state shoots in spring and SCTP Nationals each July at the Cardinal Shooting Center in Marengo, Ohio: six days of trap, skeet, sporting clays, super sporting, trap yardage and doubles. Nationals also hosts Last Competitor Standing, where 700+ kids shoot trap from the 27-yard line until one is left.

How we approach things

Coaching the whole team

Clay target shooting rewards patience, focus and practice. Here's what families can expect from ACES.

Safety is taught first

New shooters learn safe handling, range commands and etiquette before they compete. Eye and ear protection are required for everyone on the line.

Coaching for every level

Coaches work with beginners on stance, mounting the gun and following the target, and help experienced shooters fine-tune their game.

Families are part of the team

Parents and guardians are welcome at practices and shoots. Many help with travel, scoring and keeping things running.

Works alongside other teams

Already shooting for a High School Clay Target League team or with 4-H? Many of our shooters do both. More rounds means more coaching and more competition.

The ACES Score

We built our own way to measure a team

Most team scores add up the top few shooters. The ACES Score, created by our team, looks at every shooter's scores, so a team gets better when its newest shooters improve. We publish rankings for teams across the country.

Getting started

Two ways in

Every ACES shooter starts in the spring at our home range, the Wealthwood Rod & Gun Club. Which path you take depends on whether this is your first year.

First-year shooters

Community Education classes

Three two-hour sessions in March and April that teach safety and the basics. Pick the time that works: Saturday afternoons or Sunday late mornings.

  • WhoStudents in grades 5–12 who are new to the sport
  • WhenLate March to late April (2026: Saturdays 3–5 pm or Sundays 11 am–1 pm)
  • Sign upThrough Community Education, where the classes are posted each spring
Returning shooters

Advanced Class practices

For shooters in their second year and up. Four Sunday practices in March and April get everyone ready for the spring state shoots.

  • WhoGrades 5–12, second year and above
  • WhenLate March to late April, Sundays 4–7 pm
  • Cost$35 SCTP registration fee
  • Sign upRegister with the team by email

After spring classes, every ACES shooter can join summer practices and team events.

Questions from families

Frequently asked questions

Does my child need experience?

No. Many of our shooters started with ACES having never shot before. Coaches start with safety and the basics and go at each athlete's pace.

What ages can join?

ACES is for students in grades 5–12. SCTP groups athletes into divisions by grade, and our coaches help place each shooter.

Where do you practice?

Our home range is the Wealthwood Rod & Gun Club. Spring classes and practices, summer practices, the ACES Open and the ACES Shootoff are all held there.

Is it safe?

Safety is the first thing we teach and the first rule at every practice and competition. Shooters follow strict range rules under adult supervision, and eye and ear protection are required.

Do we need to buy a shotgun right away?

Please talk to us before buying anything. Coaches can help you find the right fit for your shooter and explain what equipment is needed to get started.

What does it cost?

First-year shooters sign up for the Community Education classes, and the fee is posted with the classes. Returning shooters pay a $35 SCTP registration fee for Advanced Class practices. Other costs, like shells and event entry fees, depend on how much a shooter competes. Email us and we'll walk you through a typical season.

When are practices?

Spring classes and practices run from late March through April. Summer practices are Mondays, 4–8 pm, from June through August, with extra Friday practices for shooters heading to Nationals. See the schedule above for a typical year.

Can my child shoot for ACES and another team?

Yes. Many of our shooters also shoot for their school's High School Clay Target League team or with 4-H. More rounds means more coaching and more competition.
